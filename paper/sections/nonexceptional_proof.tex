\subsection{Proof of the nonexceptional-endpoint theorem}\label{sec:nonexceptional-proof}

We now minimize the reduced objective from \Cref{cor:one-dimensional-reduction}
using the quadratic expansion in \Cref{thm:boundary-excess-expansion}.
The minimizing edge-density deficit determines the graphon optimizer and the expansions
in \Cref{thm:nonexceptional-endpoint}.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{thm:nonexceptional-endpoint}]
Let $I$, $\delta_0$, and $\eta$ be as in \Cref{cor:one-dimensional-reduction}.
After shrinking $I$ around $r_0$ and decreasing $\delta_0$, let $G$ be the
analytic extension from \Cref{thm:boundary-excess-expansion} and set
$a_0:=\inf_{r\in I}A_H(r)>0$.  Its expansion gives
$G(r,0)=\partial_\delta G(r,0)=0$ and $\partial_\delta^2G(r,0)=A_H(r)$.
By continuity and compactness of $\bar I$, decrease $\delta_0$ so that
$0<\delta_0<\inf_{r\in I}r$ and $\partial_\delta^2G(r,\delta)\ge a_0/2$
for $r\in I$ and $|\delta|<\delta_0$.
Decrease $\eta$ as needed to retain \Cref{cor:one-dimensional-reduction} with this
choice of $\delta_0$.

For $r\in I$, $\pc(r)-\eta<p<\pc(r)$, and $0<\delta<\delta_0$,
the relative entropy identity \eqref{eq:relative-entropy-identity},
applied to $B_{r-\delta,r^m}$ and the constant graphon $W\equiv r$, gives
\[
  I_{p,r}(r-\delta)-J_p(r)
  =G(r,\delta)-\lambda(p,r)\delta.
\]
Here changing $p$ from $\pc(r)$ adds an affine function of the edge density,
and the edge-density difference is $-\delta$.
Thus \Cref{cor:one-dimensional-reduction} yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:edge-deficit-minimization}
  \Phi_H(p,r)
  =J_p(r)+\min_{0<\delta<\delta_0}
    \{G(r,\delta)-\lambda(p,r)\delta\},
\end{equation}
whose minimizers correspond bijectively to graphon optimizers up to
relabeling.  The bound on $\partial_\delta^2G$ gives strict convexity, so a
solution of the critical-point equation $\partial_\delta G(r,\delta)=\lambda(p,r)$
in $(0,\delta_0)$ is necessarily the unique minimizer.

Both sides are real-analytic and vanish at
$(p,r,\delta)=(\pc(r_0),r_0,0)$, where the $\delta$-derivative of their
difference is $\partial_\delta^2G(r_0,0)=A_H(r_0)>0$.
The analytic implicit function theorem therefore gives a locally unique
analytic solution $\delta_*(p,r)\in(-\delta_0,\delta_0)$ near $(\pc(r_0),r_0)$.
Choose $\rho>0$ and decrease $\eta$ so that
$[r_0-\rho,r_0+\rho]\subseteq I$ and
\[
  U:=\{(p,r):|p-\pc(r)|<\eta,\ |r-r_0|<\rho\}
\]
has compact closure in the analytic domain of $\delta_*$,
with $0<p<r<1$ on $\bar U$.
For $|r-r_0|<\rho$, the critical-point equation at $p=\pc(r)$ has the
solution $\delta=0$, so uniqueness gives $\delta_*(\pc(r),r)=0$.
For $(p,r)\in U$ with $p<\pc(r)$, strict monotonicity of
$\partial_\delta G(r,\cdot)$ and $\partial_\delta G(r,0)=0<\lambda(p,r)$
give $\delta_*(p,r)>0$.  This shows that $\delta_*$ is the required minimizer.

Moreover,
\[
  \lambda(p,r)
  =\int_0^{\delta_*}\partial_\delta^2G(r,t)\,dt
  \ge\frac{a_0}{2}\delta_*,
\]
so $\delta_*=O_{H,U}(\lambda(p,r))$ uniformly.
Taylor's theorem applied to $\partial_\delta G(r,\delta)$ at $\delta=0$
gives $\lambda(p,r)=A_H(r)\delta_*+O_{H,U}(\delta_*^2)$.
Since $A_H(r)\ge a_0$, this yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:optimal-deficit-expansion}
  \delta_*(p,r)
  =\frac{\lambda(p,r)}{A_H(r)}+O_{H,U}(\lambda(p,r)^2).
\end{equation}

By \Cref{cor:one-dimensional-reduction}, the optimizer is bipodal and unique up to relabeling,
given by $W_{p,r}:=B_{r-\delta_*,r^m}$ with $e(W_{p,r})=r-\delta_*$ and
$t(H,W_{p,r})=r^m$.
Its block parameters are obtained by evaluating the jointly analytic
functions constructed in the proof of \Cref{thm:boundary-excess-expansion}
at $(r,\delta_*(p,r))$.  Hence $q_{ij}$ and $c$ are analytic in $(p,r)$.

After decreasing $\eta$ if necessary, the block-size estimate in
\Cref{thm:krrs-analytic-extension} and the mean value theorem give
\[
  c(p,r)=O_{H,U}\bigl(r^m-(r-\delta_*)^m\bigr)
  =O_{H,U}(\delta_*)=O_{H,U}(\lambda(p,r)).
\]
Nonconstancy gives $c(p,r)>0$.  Since $\lambda(\pc(r),r)=0$ and
$\partial_p\lambda$ is bounded on $\bar U$, decreasing $\eta$ ensures
$c(p,r)<1/2$ uniformly.  Thus $A_{p,r}:=[0,c(p,r)]$ is the smaller block,
and its size tends to zero as $p\uparrow\pc(r)$ for every fixed $r$ with
$|r-r_0|<\rho$.

Finally, \eqref{eq:optimal-deficit-expansion} gives the edge-density
expansion in \eqref{eq:optimizer-expansion}.  Substituting it into
\eqref{eq:edge-deficit-minimization} and using
\eqref{eq:boundary-excess-expansion} gives
\begin{align*}
  \Phi_H(p,r)
  &=J_p(r)+\frac12A_H(r)\delta_*^2-\lambda(p,r)\delta_*
    +O_{H,U}(\delta_*^3)\\
  &=J_p(r)-\frac{\lambda(p,r)^2}{2A_H(r)}
    +O_{H,U}(\lambda(p,r)^3).
    \qedhere
\end{align*}
\end{proof}
